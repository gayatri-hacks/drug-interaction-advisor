MEDICAL SAFETY GUIDELINES — Drug Interaction Advisor Knowledge Base
Source: Beers Criteria 2023, FDA Drug Safety Communications, Clinical Pharmacology Standards
======================================================================================

--- ELDERLY PATIENT RULES (Age ≥ 65) ---

RULE-E01: NSAIDs in Elderly Patients
Drugs: Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Ketorolac, Diclofenac, Indomethacin
Risk: HIGH
Guideline (Beers Criteria): Avoid chronic use of oral NSAIDs in elderly patients unless
alternatives are not effective and the patient can take a proton pump inhibitor (PPI)
simultaneously. NSAIDs increase the risk of gastrointestinal bleeding, peptic ulcer
disease, and acute kidney injury in patients aged 65 and older.
Action: Flag all NSAID prescriptions for patients ≥65. Recommend acetaminophen as
first-line alternative. If NSAID required, prescribe PPI prophylaxis.

RULE-E02: Benzodiazepines in Elderly Patients
Drugs: Diazepam, Lorazepam, Alprazolam, Clonazepam, Temazepam
Risk: HIGH
Guideline (Beers Criteria): Avoid benzodiazepines for any indication in older adults.
Older adults have increased sensitivity to benzodiazepines and slower metabolism.
Associated with cognitive impairment, delirium, falls, fractures, and motor vehicle
accidents in older adults.
Action: Contraindicated as first-line. If prescribed, use lowest dose, shortest duration.

RULE-E03: Anticholinergic Drugs in Elderly Patients
Drugs: Diphenhydramine, Amitriptyline, Hydroxyzine, Oxybutynin, Tolterodine
Risk: HIGH
Guideline (Beers Criteria): Avoid in older adults. Highly anticholinergic drugs cause
confusion, dry mouth, constipation, and urinary retention. Associated with increased risk
of cognitive decline and delirium.
Action: Screen all prescriptions for anticholinergic burden. Avoid in patients ≥65.

RULE-E04: Digoxin in Elderly Heart Failure Patients
Drugs: Digoxin (Lanoxin)
Risk: MODERATE
Guideline (Beers Criteria): Avoid as first-line for atrial fibrillation or heart failure
in elderly. If used, keep dose below 0.125 mg/day. Reduced renal clearance in elderly
patients leads to digoxin accumulation and toxicity (nausea, arrhythmias).
Action: If digoxin is used in patient ≥65, verify dose ≤0.125 mg/day.

RULE-E05: Long-Acting Sulfonylureas in Elderly Diabetic Patients
Drugs: Glyburide (Glibenclamide), Chlorpropamide
Risk: HIGH
Guideline (Beers Criteria): Avoid in elderly patients. Long-acting sulfonylureas cause
prolonged hypoglycemia due to reduced renal clearance in older adults.
Action: Use shorter-acting agents (glipizide, glimepiride) if sulfonylurea required.

RULE-E06: Tramadol in Elderly Patients
Drugs: Tramadol (Ultram)
Risk: MODERATE
Guideline (Beers Criteria): Avoid in older adults. Tramadol lowers seizure threshold and
can cause serotonin syndrome when combined with serotonergic agents. Also associated with
falls and hypoglycemia.
Action: Flag tramadol prescriptions for patients ≥65. Consider alternative analgesics.

RULE-E07: Z-Drugs (Non-benzodiazepine Hypnotics) in Elderly
Drugs: Zolpidem (Ambien), Eszopiclone, Zaleplon
Risk: HIGH
Guideline (Beers Criteria): Avoid in older adults. Benzodiazepine-receptor agonists
have similar adverse effects in older adults as benzodiazepines, including motor
impairment, delirium, falls, and fractures.
Action: Recommend sleep hygiene interventions and cognitive behavioral therapy for
insomnia (CBT-I) as first-line alternatives.

--- DRUG-DRUG INTERACTION RULES ---

RULE-D01: Warfarin + NSAIDs (Aspirin, Ibuprofen, Naproxen)
Severity: HIGH
Mechanism: NSAIDs inhibit platelet aggregation AND displace warfarin from plasma
protein binding, dramatically increasing free warfarin concentration and INR.
Effect: Significantly increased risk of serious bleeding including GI hemorrhage and
intracranial bleeding.
Action: Avoid combination. If pain relief needed, use acetaminophen. Monitor INR closely
if NSAID use is unavoidable.

RULE-D02: Warfarin + Fluconazole (and other Azole Antifungals)
Severity: HIGH
Mechanism: Fluconazole inhibits CYP2C9, the primary enzyme responsible for warfarin
metabolism. Results in dramatically elevated warfarin levels and INR.
Effect: Serious bleeding risk. INR can triple within 3-5 days of starting fluconazole.
Action: Avoid combination. If required, reduce warfarin dose by 25-50% and monitor INR
every 2-3 days.

RULE-D03: SSRIs + Tramadol (Serotonin Syndrome Risk)
Severity: HIGH
Drugs: Sertraline, Fluoxetine, Paroxetine, Escitalopram + Tramadol
Mechanism: SSRIs inhibit serotonin reuptake. Tramadol also inhibits serotonin reuptake
AND is a mu-opioid agonist. Combined use causes excessive serotonergic stimulation.
Effect: Serotonin syndrome — agitation, confusion, hyperthermia, tachycardia, clonus,
seizures. Can be fatal.
Action: Contraindicated combination. Use alternative analgesic (not tramadol) in patients
on SSRIs.

RULE-D04: SSRIs + MAOIs (Absolute Contraindication)
Severity: CONTRAINDICATED
Drugs: Any SSRI + Phenelzine, Tranylcypromine, Selegiline, Linezolid, Methylene Blue
Mechanism: MAOIs block serotonin breakdown. SSRIs increase serotonin release. Combined
effect causes fatal serotonin toxicity.
Effect: Fatal serotonin syndrome.
Action: Absolutely contraindicated. Require 14-day washout after stopping MAOI before
starting SSRI. Require 5-week washout after fluoxetine.

RULE-D05: Metformin + NSAIDs in Renal Impairment
Severity: MODERATE-HIGH
Mechanism: NSAIDs reduce renal prostaglandin synthesis, decreasing GFR. This reduces
metformin clearance, causing drug accumulation and lactic acidosis risk.
Effect: Lactic acidosis — medical emergency with high mortality.
Action: Avoid NSAIDs in patients on metformin with CKD (eGFR <60). Monitor renal
function.

RULE-D06: ACE Inhibitors / ARBs + NSAIDs
Severity: MODERATE
Drugs: Lisinopril, Ramipril, Enalapril, Losartan, Valsartan + Ibuprofen, Naproxen
Mechanism: NSAIDs inhibit prostaglandin-mediated afferent arteriole dilation in kidneys,
counteracting the renoprotective effect of ACE inhibitors/ARBs. Also causes sodium
retention, reducing antihypertensive efficacy.
Effect: Reduced blood pressure control, acute kidney injury, hyperkalemia.
Action: Monitor renal function and potassium. Avoid chronic concurrent use.

RULE-D07: Digoxin + Amiodarone
Severity: HIGH
Mechanism: Amiodarone inhibits P-glycoprotein, the primary efflux transporter for
digoxin. Results in 1.5-2x increase in serum digoxin levels.
Effect: Digoxin toxicity — nausea, bradycardia, heart block, ventricular arrhythmias.
Action: Reduce digoxin dose by 30-50% when starting amiodarone. Monitor digoxin levels.

RULE-D08: Sildenafil/Tadalafil + Nitrates (Absolute Contraindication)
Severity: CONTRAINDICATED
Drugs: Sildenafil, Tadalafil, Vardenafil + Isosorbide mononitrate, Isosorbide dinitrate,
Glyceryl trinitrate (GTN/Nitroglycerin), Amyl nitrite
Mechanism: Both PDE5 inhibitors and nitrates cause vasodilation via cGMP pathway.
Combined effect results in massive, uncontrolled vasodilation.
Effect: Life-threatening hypotension, syncope, myocardial infarction, death.
Action: Absolutely contraindicated. Never prescribe together.

RULE-D09: Lithium + NSAIDs
Severity: HIGH
Mechanism: NSAIDs reduce renal prostaglandin synthesis, decreasing GFR and lithium
clearance. Lithium accumulates to toxic levels.
Effect: Lithium toxicity — tremor, confusion, cardiac arrhythmias, seizures, renal failure.
Action: Avoid NSAIDs in lithium patients. If pain relief needed, use acetaminophen.
Monitor lithium levels if NSAID is unavoidable.

RULE-D10: Simvastatin/Lovastatin + CYP3A4 Inhibitors
Severity: HIGH
Drugs: Simvastatin, Lovastatin + Amiodarone, Clarithromycin, Ketoconazole, Fluconazole,
Erythromycin, Grapefruit juice (high quantities)
Mechanism: CYP3A4 inhibitors block metabolism of simvastatin/lovastatin, causing
significant accumulation.
Effect: Myopathy and rhabdomyolysis — muscle breakdown leading to kidney failure.
Action: Avoid combination. Use rosuvastatin or pravastatin (not CYP3A4-metabolized)
as alternatives.

RULE-D11: Azithromycin + QT-Prolonging Drugs
Severity: HIGH
Drugs: Azithromycin + Hydroxychloroquine, Haloperidol, Methadone, Sotalol, Amiodarone,
Cisapride, Ondansetron
Mechanism: Additive inhibition of cardiac hERG potassium channels, prolonging QT interval.
Effect: Torsades de pointes — polymorphic ventricular tachycardia, cardiac arrest.
Action: Avoid combinations. Obtain baseline ECG and monitor QTc interval.

RULE-D12: Methotrexate + NSAIDs
Severity: HIGH
Mechanism: NSAIDs compete with methotrexate for renal tubular secretion via organic
anion transporters, reducing methotrexate clearance and causing accumulation.
Effect: Methotrexate toxicity — bone marrow suppression, mucositis, hepatotoxicity.
Action: Avoid combination. If NSAID required, reduce methotrexate dose and monitor CBC.

--- RENAL IMPAIRMENT RULES ---

RULE-R01: NSAIDs in Chronic Kidney Disease
Risk: HIGH
Guideline: NSAIDs are relatively contraindicated in patients with CKD (eGFR <60).
They reduce renal prostaglandin synthesis, causing vasoconstriction of the afferent
arteriole and reducing GFR. Can precipitate acute-on-chronic kidney injury.
Action: Avoid all NSAIDs in CKD patients. Acetaminophen is the preferred analgesic.

RULE-R02: Metformin in Renal Impairment
Risk: HIGH
Guideline (FDA): Metformin is contraindicated when eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m². Use with
caution when eGFR 30-45. Risk of lactic acidosis increases proportionally with degree
of renal impairment.
Action: Check eGFR before prescribing. Dose reduce at eGFR 30-45. Discontinue if eGFR
drops below 30.

RULE-R03: Aminoglycosides in Renal Impairment
Drugs: Gentamicin, Tobramycin, Amikacin
Risk: HIGH
Guideline: Aminoglycosides are nephrotoxic and ototoxic. Require significant dose
adjustment based on renal function. TDM (therapeutic drug monitoring) mandatory.
Action: Calculate dose adjustment using Cockcroft-Gault equation. Monitor serum levels.

--- ALLERGY CROSS-REACTIVITY RULES ---

RULE-A01: Penicillin Allergy — Beta-Lactam Cross-Reactivity
Risk: HIGH
Guideline: Patients with true penicillin allergy (IgE-mediated) may cross-react with
cephalosporins (5-10% cross-reactivity) due to similar beta-lactam ring structures.
Amoxicillin, ampicillin, piperacillin, and other penicillins are absolutely
contraindicated. Cross-reactivity with cephalosporins is based on R1 side chain
similarity.
Action: Avoid all penicillins. Use with caution for cephalosporins, assess risk-benefit.
Consider skin testing before using cephalosporins in high-risk patients.

RULE-A02: NSAID Hypersensitivity — COX-1 Cross-Reactivity
Risk: HIGH
Guideline: Patients with aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD) or aspirin/NSAID
urticaria may react to any non-selective COX-1 inhibitor.
Cross-reactive drugs: Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Ketorolac, Indomethacin, Diclofenac.
COX-2 selective inhibitors (celecoxib) may be tolerated in some patients.
Action: Screen for aspirin allergy/intolerance before prescribing any NSAID.

RULE-A03: Sulfonamide Antibiotic Allergy
Risk: MODERATE
Guideline: True allergy to sulfonamide antibiotics (sulfamethoxazole) may predict
reaction to non-antibiotic sulfonamides. Cross-reactivity is debated but clinically
relevant for furosemide, thiazide diuretics, and celecoxib in high-sensitivity patients.
Action: Exercise caution when prescribing sulfonamide-containing non-antibiotics to
patients with sulfonamide antibiotic allergy.

--- CARDIAC SAFETY RULES ---

RULE-C01: Heart Failure + NSAIDs
Risk: HIGH
Guideline (ACC/AHA): NSAIDs are relatively contraindicated in patients with heart failure.
They promote sodium and water retention, worsen fluid overload, and blunt the efficacy
of diuretics and ACE inhibitors/ARBs.
Action: Avoid NSAIDs in heart failure patients. Acetaminophen is preferred.

RULE-C02: Non-Dihydropyridine CCBs in HFrEF
Drugs: Verapamil, Diltiazem
Risk: HIGH
Guideline (ACC/AHA): Verapamil and diltiazem are contraindicated in HFrEF (ejection
fraction <40%). Their negative inotropic effects worsen left ventricular function.
Action: Use amlodipine or felodipine (dihydropyridine CCBs) if CCB required in HFrEF.

RULE-C03: Beta-Blockers + Verapamil/Diltiazem Combination
Risk: HIGH
Guideline: Combining beta-blockers with non-dihydropyridine CCBs causes additive
suppression of AV nodal conduction. Can result in complete heart block or severe
bradycardia requiring pacemaker.
Action: Avoid concurrent use. If required, use only under cardiology supervision with
continuous monitoring.

--- DIABETES-SPECIFIC RULES ---

RULE-DB01: Corticosteroids in Diabetic Patients
Drugs: Prednisone, Dexamethasone, Methylprednisolone, Prednisolone
Risk: MODERATE
Guideline: Corticosteroids cause dose-dependent hyperglycemia through gluconeogenesis
stimulation and insulin resistance. Effect is most pronounced in the afternoon/evening
with morning dosing.
Action: Increase blood glucose monitoring frequency. Adjust diabetes medications.
Consider insulin sliding scale in hospital settings.

RULE-DB02: Fluoroquinolones in Diabetic Patients
Drugs: Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin
Risk: MODERATE
Guideline (FDA Warning): Fluoroquinolones can cause both hypoglycemia (especially
with insulin or sulfonylureas) and hyperglycemia. Gatifloxacin is most problematic
and should be avoided.
Action: Monitor blood glucose closely during fluoroquinolone therapy in diabetic patients.

--- GENERAL PRESCRIBING SAFETY PRINCIPLES ---

PRINCIPLE-1: Polypharmacy Risk
Patients on 5 or more medications are at significantly increased risk of adverse drug
reactions and drug-drug interactions. Each additional drug increases interaction
probability by approximately 10%.
Action: Review entire medication list at each visit. Apply deprescribing principles.

PRINCIPLE-2: Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs
The following drugs require extra vigilance: Warfarin, Digoxin, Lithium, Theophylline,
Phenytoin, Methotrexate, Cyclosporine, Tacrolimus, Aminoglycosides.
Any drug interaction involving these agents must be treated as high priority.
Action: Therapeutic drug monitoring mandatory. Extra caution with any new co-prescription.

PRINCIPLE-3: Renal and Hepatic Dose Adjustment
Always check renal function (eGFR/creatinine) and liver function (ALT, bilirubin)
before prescribing drugs with significant renal or hepatic elimination. Refer to
prescribing information for dose adjustments.
Action: Calculate eGFR using CKD-EPI equation. Adjust doses accordingly.

PRINCIPLE-4: Pregnancy and Lactation Safety
Category X and CONTRAINDICATED drugs in pregnancy include: ACE inhibitors (2nd/3rd
trimester), Warfarin (1st trimester), NSAIDs (3rd trimester), Methotrexate, Thalidomide,
Isotretinoin, Valproate (teratogenic).
Action: Always screen for pregnancy before prescribing high-risk medications.
